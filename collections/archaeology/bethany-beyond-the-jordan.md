# Bethany beyond the Jordan (Al-Maghtas): the baptism site

Al-Maghtas, on the east bank of the Jordan in Jordan, is the principal
traditional location of Jesus's baptism by John. It has been excavated as an
archaeological site, not merely venerated as one.

## What the excavations found

Roman and Byzantine remains: churches, monastic cells, pilgrim installations,
water channels and baptismal pools, with occupation and building phases from
roughly the Roman period through the Byzantine and into the Islamic era. The
inscribed and structural evidence points to continuous Christian commemoration
across centuries — the site was built up because people came to it, again and
again, for a very long time.

UNESCO inscribed it in 2015 as "Bethany Beyond the Jordan (Al-Maghtas)" for
exactly that reason: the remains testify to a durable tradition of pilgrimage
and baptism, not to a single datable event.

## What it establishes, and what it does not

It establishes that the east-bank Jordan location was commemorated as the
baptism site from at latest the fourth century, with substantial infrastructure
built for the purpose. It does not identify the precise stretch of river where
John stood. The Jordan's course has shifted, later buildings are superimposed on
earlier ones, and pilgrimage geography tends to consolidate around one convenient
site rather than preserve a fixed point in a meandering river.

The Fourth Gospel also names Aenon near Salim as a place where John was baptizing
(John 3:23), and its location is disputed. Any map of John's activity should
distinguish places the texts name from places the later tradition confidently
identified.
