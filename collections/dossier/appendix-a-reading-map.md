# Appendix A. Primary-source reading map

This index separates the surviving ancient sources from later interpretations. A passage's presence establishes what its author reports; it does not independently verify every event described. Read parallel Gospel accounts separately before combining them. Matthew and Luke reuse Mark, and Luke-Acts forms one author's two-volume narrative, so repeated material is not automatically independent corroboration.

For the scriptural framework, compare Isaiah 40:3-5; Malachi 3:1 and 4:5-6; and 2 Kings 1:8. These are earlier texts subsequently applied to John. They do not name him. Malachi 4:5-6 corresponds to 3:23-24 in Jewish chapter numbering. In all these readings, distinguish John the Baptist from other New Testament figures named John.

## Josephus

Read Josephus, Antiquities 18.116-119, corresponding to Book XVIII, chapter 5, section 2 in Whiston's translation[S82]. This is the essential non-Christian account: John's moral teaching, immersion, popular influence, Antipas's fear, imprisonment, and death at Machaerus. Read the surrounding chapter for the war with Aretas and Herodian family relationships. Whiston's eighteenth-century explanatory footnotes are not Josephus's own words; some harmonize his narrative with the Gospels.

## Qur'an

The principal passages concerning Yaḥyā are Qur'an 3:38-41[S83], announcing his birth and prophetic character; 19:1-15[S64], recounting Zakariyya's prayer and describing Yaḥyā's wisdom and righteousness; 6:85[S63], listing him among righteous figures; and 21:89-90[S69], describing the answer to his father's prayer. Later commentaries and prophetic legends expand these notices and should be identified separately.

## Early Christian noncanonical index

Gospel of Thomas 46[S84]: praise of John combined with the

- claim that becoming little and knowing the kingdom surpasses him. An awkward phrase about eyes has differing translations.

- Gospel of the Hebrews / Gospel of the Nazarenes: scholarly fragment guide[S85]: fragments associated with Jerome discuss Jesus's need for baptism and the Spirit resting upon him. Their assignment among separate Jewish-Christian gospels is disputed; no complete text survives. Gospel of the Ebionites: fragments and source guide[S86]:

- excerpts preserved by Epiphanius alter John's diet and expand Jesus's baptism. They illuminate later vegetarian and theological interpretations. Protevangelium of James 22-24[S87]: Elizabeth and John

- escape Herod through a miraculously opened mountain; Zechariah is murdered. These stories are absent from Luke. Pseudo-Clementine Recognitions 1.54 and 1.60[S88]: depicts

- followers claiming John is the Christ. This is later controversial literature, not a contemporary transcript.
