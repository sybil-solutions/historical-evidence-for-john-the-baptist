# Machaerus: the fortress on the ridge

Machaerus is a Herodian palace-fortress on a ridge east of the Dead Sea, in
present-day Jordan. Josephus places John's imprisonment and execution there, and
the site is the one location in the story that archaeology can actually inspect.

## What the site contains

Herodian masonry, a fortified wall circuit, a palace wing, cisterns, a bath
house and a courtyard large enough for a royal gathering. Excavation has run in
campaigns since the 1970s, with the most recent work directed by Győző Vörös
under the American Center of Research in Amman. The architectural sequence —
Hasmonean, Herodian, later Roman — is published in excavation reports rather
than inferred from the literary text.

## What it establishes, and what it does not

It establishes that the setting of the Gospel and Josephan narrative existed as
a real Herodian stronghold, furnished and defensible, in the right period and
the right region. It does not identify a prison cell, a banquet hall where a
dance took place, or a burial place. Those are readings of the texts laid over
the stones; a room big enough for a royal party preserves no record of what was
said in it. The frequent labels — "Salome's dancing floor", "John's cell" — are
interpretive, and the excavation reports do not authorize them.

Josephus adds a detail the Gospels do not: after Antipas's army was destroyed by
Aretas, popular opinion read the defeat as divine retribution for the execution
of John. That is a statement about how the death was remembered, not a record of
the execution itself.
