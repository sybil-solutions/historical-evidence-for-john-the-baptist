# The "cave of John the Baptist" near Suba

In 2004 the archaeologist Shimon Gibson published a cave on Kibbutz Tzuba, west
of Jerusalem, proposing it as a place John used. The identification is the most
contested item in this collection.

## What is in the cave

A rock-cut installation with a plastered basin and channels, a carved standing
figure holding a staff, and Iron Age and later pottery. Gibson's reading is that
the basin and channels served a purification ritual, that the figure is a
schematic holy man, and that Byzantine-era inscriptions and pilgrimage use show
later veneration of the spot.

## Why the identification is disputed

James F. Strange's review, in *Biblical Archaeology Review*, accepted the
interest of the ritual installation while rejecting the chain of inference: an
uninscribed figure cannot be identified as John, plastered basins of this kind
are not unique, and later veneration would demonstrate only that later
communities believed something about the cave — not what happened in it in the
first century. The gaps between "ritual installation", "figure", "John" and
"this cave" are each filled by argument, not by finds.

The monograph remains useful as a report on the installation. It is not evidence
that John lived in, taught in, or baptized anyone in that cave.
