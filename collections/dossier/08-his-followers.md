# 8. What became of his followers?

John's followers did not simply disappear at the moment he baptized Jesus. The Gospels describe a distinguishable circle with prayer and fasting practices, concern for their teacher and continuing interaction with Jesus. Some may have joined Jesus's movement. The Fourth Gospel explicitly narrates such a transfer in the case of Andrew and another disciple.

Acts gives two important later scenes. Apollos is an Alexandrian Jew who knows John's baptism yet already teaches about Jesus; Priscilla and Aquila instruct him more fully. In Ephesus, about twelve people described as disciples know John's baptism and subsequently receive baptism in Jesus's name and the Spirit. These scenes suggest continued circulation of John's baptism, but do not prove a large, entirely separate church of John spanning the empire. [S38]

The ambiguity is historical information. Boundaries between Baptist adherents, Jesus followers and people with incomplete instruction may not have been as tidy as later religious categories imply. Acts also has its own theological purpose: it integrates these people into the church's account of Spirit-filled mission.

Later Pseudo-Clementine literature depicts people who considered John the Messiah and connects his circle with Simon Magus and Dositheus. Such texts show that John's authority remained available for arguments about legitimate religious succession. Their late, polemical character prevents treating their organizational genealogies as a reliable first-century register. Appendix A identifies the relevant passages.
