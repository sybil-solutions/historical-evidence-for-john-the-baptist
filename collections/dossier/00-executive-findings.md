# Executive findings

John mattered first because people believed he spoke with prophetic authority, and because his call to repentance attracted a following that a ruler considered dangerous. He matters to the history of Christianity because Jesus received his baptism and emerged in close relationship to his mission. He matters to Christian theology because the Gospels interpret him as the person who connects Israel's scriptural expectations to Jesus. His importance extends beyond Christianity: Islam honors him as Yahya, and Mandaeans preserve a different religious memory in which he is an exemplary teacher rather than a subordinate witness to Jesus. These are related histories, but they cannot simply be combined into one biography.

The strongest historical reconstruction is of a Jewish preacher and baptizer, active in the early first century, who called people to moral renewal, attracted disciples, baptized Jesus, and was executed by Herod Antipas. Some elements have more support than others. Josephus independently attests the teacher, his influence, baptism and execution; the Jesus connection rests on Christian sources. The surviving evidence is much thinner for his family, education, private thoughts, precise calendar dates and exact words. [S01] [S02]

Seven findings guide this report:

- John had significance independently of Jesus. Josephus's account makes sense without mentioning Christianity.

- Jesus's baptism is more secure than a detailed apprenticeship. Participation is strongly supported; the duration and content of Jesus's association with John are not documented.
