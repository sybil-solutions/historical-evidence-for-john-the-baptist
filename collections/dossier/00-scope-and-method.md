- John's message joined judgment to practical ethics. The sources describe changed conduct, generosity and justice, as well as warnings of divine intervention.

- His baptism developed Jewish practices. Claims that he invented water purification, inward repentance or forgiveness outside sacrifice oversimplify ancient Judaism.

- The Gospels honor and subordinate him. Their literary treatments differ and should be compared before being harmonized.

- Neither a secret Gnostic founder nor a lifelong rival of Jesus is established. Such reconstructions require arguments beyond the primary evidence.

- Archaeology illuminates his setting and later cult. No remains, inscription or personal writing has been securely authenticated as John's own.

The chapters below supply the evidence and qualifications behind these judgments. The final assessment explains why a man with so little surviving biography became central to several religious traditions.

## Scope and method

This report surveys the principal surviving sources, the major historical disputes and the most consequential later interpretations. It does not claim to inventory every sermon, artwork or publication ever produced about John. Research was checked on 27 October 2026. Biblical references identify what a text says; they do not automatically certify that every narrated event occurred. Modern scholarship is identified by author where interpretations differ. A source guide distinguishes primary texts, research, institutional descriptions and author summaries.
