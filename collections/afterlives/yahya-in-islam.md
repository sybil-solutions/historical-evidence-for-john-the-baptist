# Yahya in Islam

In the Qur'an, John is Yahya, son of Zakariyya: a prophet in his own right,
promised to an aged father, honoured as chaste and righteous, and given wisdom
in childhood. Four surahs carry the material, and they are the primary record of
the Islamic John.

## What the Qur'an says

- **3:38-41** — Zakariyya's prayer in the sanctuary, the announcement of a son
  named Yahya, "a word from God", noble, chaste, a prophet among the righteous.
- **6:85** — Yahya grouped with Zakariyya, Jesus and Elias among the righteous.
- **19:2-15** — the extended annunciation scene; Yahya given judgement as a
  child, compassion, purity, devotion, and peace on the day he is born, dies and
  is raised.
- **21:89-90** — Zakariyya's prayer answered; the family hastening in good
  works.

The Qur'an does not narrate John baptizing Jesus, and it does not carry the
Gospel story of the dance, the platter and the execution. Calling him "John the
Baptist" in English imports a role the Qur'anic text does not give him. Surah
19's emphases — birth, righteousness, peace at death — are the Islamic account's
own priorities.

## What later tradition adds

Ḥadīth material integrates him into Islamic teaching: Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 3207
places Yahya and Jesus together in the second heaven during the night journey,
and Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhī 2863 attributes five injunctions to him — worship without
partners, prayer, fasting, charity, remembrance of God. These are later
religious witnesses to his authority, not contemporary records of his sermons.

The Umayyad Mosque in Damascus contains a shrine associated with Yahya, on the
site of a church of John. The building and the tradition are demonstrable; the
identity of the relic is a separate question.
