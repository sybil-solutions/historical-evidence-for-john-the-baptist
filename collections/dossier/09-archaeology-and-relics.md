# 9. Archaeology, relics and claims of proof

## Machaerus confirms a setting

The palace-fortress of Machaerus, in present-day Jordan, is a real archaeological site with substantial Herodian remains and published excavation reports. Josephus supplies the literary connection to John's imprisonment and execution. Excavation independently establishes the setting, but it does not identify John through an inscribed prison record or a named skeleton. [S39]

Claims that a particular niche was Antipas's throne or a courtyard was the precise place where the dancer performed remain more specific than the evidence warrants. An architectural identification and an event identification are different achievements. A room suitable for a royal gathering does not preserve a record of what was said or done there.

## The baptism site confirms a history of devotion

Al-Maghtas, or Bethany beyond the Jordan, is a major traditional location of Jesus's baptism. UNESCO describes Roman and Byzantine remains, churches, pools, hermit activity and a history of pilgrimage, with substantial commemorative evidence from the fourth century onward. Those are valuable findings. World Heritage status does not independently prove the exact point in the river where John baptized Jesus. [S40]

The Fourth Gospel also locates activity at Aenon near Salim; the exact site is disputed. River courses, place names and later pilgrimage traditions complicate attempts to draw a precise itinerary for John. A map should distinguish textually named locations from confident modern identifications.

## The Bulgarian bones: what the actual study found

Human and animal remains were discovered in a reliquary context on the island of Sveti Ivan near Sozopol, Bulgaria. A related inscription refers to Saint John and his feast. In the final published scientific study, one human metacarpal produced a radiocarbon date of 5-75 calibrated CE at 68.2% probability. That is compatible with John's period but does not identify an individual.

The DNA results are more cautionary than popular early reports suggested. The samples yielded very little human DNA, with none of the expected ancient-DNA damage patterns. The authors concluded that the obtained sequences most likely represented modern contamination. The study therefore does not provide an authenticated genetic profile of John. [S41]

## A cave associated with John is not an identified home

Shimon Gibson proposed linking a cave near Suba to John. Archaeologist James F. Strange's review acknowledges the interesting ritual remains while emphasizing the circumstantial nature of the link. An incised human figure has no identifying name; later veneration would not prove that John personally used the cave. [S42]

## Was he an Essene from Qumran?

The comparison is understandable: wilderness, purification, moral demands, scriptural interpretation and eschatological expectation occur in both discussions. Yet similarities within a shared Jewish culture do not establish membership in one settlement. No securely identified scroll names the Baptist as its author or its Teacher of Righteousness.

Jeffrey García's reassessment challenges assumptions about geographical proximity and identical uses of Isaiah 40:3. Adinolfi and Taylor likewise argue that the proposed connections are broader Second Temple patterns, not proof of Qumran affiliation. John may have encountered related ideas; a childhood at Qumran or expulsion from an Essene community remains a reconstruction. [S43]

## Evidence checklist

| Claim | Best assessment |
|---|---|
| John existed and led a baptismal movement | Strong historical conclusion |
| Antipas ordered his execution | Strong historical conclusion |
| Jesus received John's baptism | Widely accepted historical conclusion |
| John belonged to Qumran | Unproved hypothesis |
| A traditional shrine marks the exact first-century event | Requires evidence beyond later commemoration |
| Radiocarbon dating has identified John's bones | False: age compatibility is not personal identification |
| DNA has established a genetic profile of John | Unsupported by the published relic study |
| A later book preserves John's own verbatim teachings | Must be argued passage by passage; no authenticated autograph exists |
